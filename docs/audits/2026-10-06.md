# Arcana revival audit — 2026-10-06

> Dated and never required reading. The active plan is maintained outside the repository; this document is the evidence it cites. The previous audit is `docs/audit-2026-08.md`.

## Charge

Revisit the project after two idle months, with substantially more compute and stronger coding agents available: audit what is here and what state it is in, confirm whether work was stranded on a branch, and report. Five read-only subagent audits (engine, web, AI/RL, card generation, docs and hygiene) plus the parent session's own build, test and bench runs.

## Argument

The repository is sound at the foundation and its own last plan is stale in three places, so the revival should start from this pass rather than from `docs/audit-2026-08.md`. The evidence for soundness is a run, not a reading: `cargo build --workspace` finishes clean on rustc 1.99.0 (169 warnings, all the known `unreachable pattern` codegen shape in `arcana-cards`), and `cargo test --workspace --no-fail-fast` passes 1,590 tests with 0 failures and 46 ignored in 3 min 18 s on oneiros, 5.1 GB peak resident. The branch worry is moot: `main`, `origin/main` and `origin/cardgen-subagent-pipeline` all resolve to `98e61559`; the August session fast-forwarded `main` by 540 commits and the branch has been redundant since. Nothing is stashed and the tree is clean.

The August plan is stale first about the catalog. `ROADMAP.md` §2 lays out a Wave 1 of about 3,950 cards needing no engine work and a Wave 3 of about 15,800 multi-ability creatures, and the August audit parked that plan as future work. The git history shows all of it landed in June: the five Wave 1 shapes on 2026-06-10 (`5b2847f4`), Aura and Planeswalker on 06-15, `MultiAbilityCreature` on 06-16, and by 06-23 (`c6251589`) that bucket was declared exhausted, taking the catalog from 8.4k to 20.3k in thirteen days. A pilot on 2026-06-27 (`bebe3bf1`) measured what is left: of 4,086 net-new supported prompts, an 80-card spell sample produced 75 engine-gap stubs and 5 passes, 15 percent after prompt repair. The remaining roughly 10k cards of the 30.9k vintage-legal corpus are therefore engine work (multi-ability spells, modal and X spells, Vehicles, activated-ability enchantments, the morph and foretell family of cast paths), not generation. The pipeline itself has no in-repo driver: `arcana-gen` dumps prompts and verifies output, and generation was a Claude Code session dispatching fleets of subagents (`arcana-gen/scripts/RUNBOOK.md`), which is exactly what a current session can still do. Two quality findings attach: the behavioral allowlist grew from 417 to 479 across the Wave 3 batches against its own header rule, and stale allowlist entries only print a note rather than fail, so the ratchet cannot be forced to tighten (`arcana-cards/src/lib.rs:1403-1409`); and the probe exercises only spells, triggers and activated abilities, so static, replacement and keyword behavior is checked only structurally.

The plan is stale second about the research line. `docs/rl-benchmark.md` closes three pre-registered experiments as nulls, and the August audit froze the line on that basis. Read against the code, each null was a linear value over 123 aggregate features fitted on about 30 self-play games per deck, evaluated at 12 games per pair on a five-deck capsule (`docs/rl-benchmark/capsule-*.txt`); the document's own learning-curve diagnostic says about 150 games were needed to match the hand-tuned material leaf's log-loss. The identity experiment added 285 features to a model fitted on a few hundred rows. These are compute- and data-starved negatives, and the only durable conclusion is that a cheap linear leaf over aggregates is near its ceiling. Separately, every capsule number predates the A-2 fix of 2026-08-04 (`4aaef49f`), which removed a prefix-truncation bias from PIMC's candidate set that could drop whole-board attack declarations; the headline that material-leaf Monte Carlo beats PIMC may be partly that artifact, and the re-baseline the August log says is owed was never run. The deck-evaluation arc divides: its internal Goodhart finding (an optimized deck inverting under a stronger referee, `docs/capsule-pioneer/results.txt`) is a true negative worth keeping, and its external-validation null is data-limited by a censored top-8 target with four or five buckets, which compute cannot repair. What exists for the named successor, PIMC action distillation, is `PimcPolicy::score_actions` (`arcana-ai/src/search.rs:930`) and nothing downstream: `action_flattening.rs` is a four-line stub, the `Policy` trait sees only a 123-float state and an unordered legal list, and there is no dataset writer or trainer. That connection between the nulls and their sample sizes is my inference; the documents call the results closed.

The plan is stale third about the client's readiness. Two defects undo work the August log records as shipped. The deck store (2.2, `c216046d`) keys the browser profile under `arcana.profile` as a raw UUID (`arcana-web/static/app.js:552-556`), and the lobby page writes the same key as `{"name":…}` (`static/stage.html:273-274`) when a player names themselves; the id then fails the server's charset check and sync dies silently, because the client ignores the 400. Verified by reading both files. And `drawTargetingArrows` is declared twice in `static/index.html` (a real implementation at 1623, a no-op "placeholder" at 2195); declarations hoist and the last wins, so arrows never draw. Beyond those, the HTTP layer still has no tests (`src/main.rs`, 1,585 lines, none), the claimed 8 MiB store cap is really axum's 2 MiB default with no body-limit layer, and the local-only guard trusts the connecting peer, so any loopback reverse proxy would expose the solo routes. Those last items matter only off the LAN.

Two engine findings are new. The last-known-information map `lki` is declared with `std::collections::HashMap` (`arcana-core/src/state.rs:19,184`) while `collections.rs` mandates the Fx aliases precisely so that iteration order is stable across processes; trigger collection extends its source list from `state.lki.iter()` (`engine.rs:2600-2601`). Networked matches resume by replaying a transcript in a fresh process, so an LKI-sourced simultaneous-trigger order that differs across processes would diverge a resume. Whether the downstream APNAP re-sort fully masks it was not traced; the import is verified, the consequence is likely. `combat.rs`, `deck.rs` and `types.rs` carry the same std import. The second is the C-2 scoping the August plan called its keystone: the bare function pointers are plain `fn` items with no captures (no `Box<dyn>` anywhere in core), so a name table is straightforward, but `Effect` and about 100 other reachable types carry no serde derives at all and `StackEntry` already skips fields a round trip would lose. The honest size is one to two weeks of agent work in core plus a mechanical codemod over about 230 card files that construct pointers directly; the 12.7k `TriggeredAbilityDef` literals live in the registry, not the state, and need no change. That estimate is inferred from counts, not from an attempt.

Hygiene is the cheapest finding and the most public. `levineuwirth/arcana` is a public GitHub repository with 2 stars, no LICENSE (`licenseInfo: null`), no CI, no `rust-toolchain.toml`, no workspace `edition` or `rust-version`, and `.gitignore` entries that would silently block any paper (`*.pdf`, `*.tex`). The 1.2 MB generated `register_all.rs` is rewritten on every card landing and is every one of the fifteen largest blobs in history. 634 of 641 commits carry a `Co-Authored-By: Claude` trailer, the reverse of the no-trailer rule pmacs and proof-broker settled on. `kaggle.zip` is absent from disk, so the gauntlet analysis scripts in `arcana-ai/scripts/` cannot run from a fresh clone without re-downloading it.

Throughput has still never been measured. `arcana-core/benches/simulation.rs` exists with criterion and names the spec targets (more than 20k games per second, clone under 5 µs, legal actions under 50 µs) on a seed-card deck; no number is recorded anywhere in the repository. The claim stands as the README states it.

What follows for the revival, as my recommendation. The client is one fix round from the August goal: the two frontend bugs, a body-limit layer, and handler tests. The research line is reopenable at near-zero engineering cost because the three ready experiments need only cluster time: the A-2 strength re-baseline head-to-head at PIMC budgets the laptop could not afford (PIMC at 16/160 on 32 decks was about fifteen hours there, `docs/gauntlet-results/control-arm-PI.txt:79`), the same three capsule experiments at hundreds to thousands of games per deck with a nonlinear leaf, and the criterion bench on a catalog deck as a gate on anything larger. DTU's cluster is many independent CPU cores under LSF with working `rustup` and no Rust module (DTU HPC mathematical and scientific computing (2026-10-01)), which is the shape of all three. Distillation proper and any self-play loop are blocked on an action encoder and on catalog access from Python, not on C-2. Catalog growth is engine work first and should wait for a ruling on whether the engine's cast-path gaps are worth closing. The ordering among the three is the author's.

## Coverage

The parent session ran the workspace build and the full test suite, fetched the remote, and verified by reading the source the three findings that carry the most weight: the `arcana.profile` collision, the duplicate `drawTargetingArrows`, and the std `HashMap` import in `state.rs`. Not done: no card file was read for quality beyond what the agents sampled; the C-2 size is an estimate from grep counts; the determinism consequence was not traced past the trigger-collection site; no `#[ignore]` test ran, so the live Scryfall download and the cargo-spawning verify tests are unexercised since June; `arcana-py` was not built with maturin; nothing ran on DTU. The subagents' line citations were spot-checked, not all re-read.
